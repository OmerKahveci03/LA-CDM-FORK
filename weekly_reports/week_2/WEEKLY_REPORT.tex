\section{Project Objective}

This week was primarily spent attempting to start the full LA-CDM training
run. The replication did not produce a completed trained model or new
evaluation results.

\section{Accomplishments}

I submitted several training runs on HiPerGator using two NVIDIA L4 GPUs.
The model and vLLM process initialized successfully, but training repeatedly
ran out of GPU memory. One run reached step 90 of 300 before failing during
backpropagation. Another configuration failed during vLLM initialization
because its memory reservation was too small for the model weights.

I reduced GRPO generations from eight to four, reduced the maximum completion
length from 256 to 128 tokens, and retained microbatching and chunked
reference-log-probability calculations. These changes have not yet been
validated by a completed full training run.

\section{Challenges}

The main issue was GPU memory. Two L4 GPUs provide separate 22~GB memory
spaces rather than one combined pool: one GPU is used for training and the
other for vLLM. The training GPU still exceeded its capacity on longer
examples. A larger GPU would likely simplify the run, but an A40 was not
available in the selected partition.

\section{Next Steps}

Given the resource limitations encountered during training, I have decided to
pivot away from working toward reproducing the paper's results. During the
next week, while my other teammates focus on reviewing the literature and
continuing their reproduction efforts, I will begin building a full-stack
application to support our agentic-search work.

Since our group has become interested in the OMOP data model, I will use an
OMOP-formatted MIMIC dataset that I already have access to through PhysioNet.
The application will allow a user to submit a free-text request alongside a
patient ID. A search agent will then retrieve relevant information for the
frontend to visualize. As we continue exploring the project's options, the
scope may evolve.
